The Plant-Herbivore Interaction & Defense Simulator (PHIDS) serves a dual purpose. While its primary surface mechanics are grounded in exact theoretical ecology and peer-reviewed biological kinetics (e.g., Holling Type II curves, Hill equations, mycorrhizal topology), the engine's mathematical architecture serves as a bio-inspired framework for distributed, cooperative security in adaptive, resource-constrained IoT networks - with a particular focus on large-scale static Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs).

This mathematical duality enables the simulator to execute **Evolutionary Encapsulated Design Space Exploration (EEDSE)**, optimizing dynamic security schemes for highly constrained environments. The conceptual foundation for this approach was recently presented at the *International Conference on Membrane Computing (ICMC 2026)*. While the published paper focused on an initial, unrelated simulation attempt, the PHIDS engine was teased during the conference presentation as the next-generation architecture for EEDSE, generating great interest among attendees.

## Application: Evolutionary Design Space Exploration (EEDSE)

By viewing the simulation through the lens of WSNs, **Evolutionary Encapsulated Design Space Exploration** can be applied to discover Pareto-optimal configurations.

In a WSN, nodes have finite battery life and minimal computational bandwidth. Constantly evaluating cryptographic signatures or maintaining complex firewalls (Constitutive Defenses) quickly drains node batteries. Conversely, relying purely on reactive, localized threat isolation (Inducible Defenses) introduces response latency, risking total node compromise before a defense is activated.

PHIDS allows cybersecurity architects to simulate massive, multi-generational parameter sweeps. By tuning parameters such as `handling_time`, `growth_rate` (node recharge), and `trigger_thresholds` across the $1024 \times 1024$ grid, the EEDSE pipeline discovers configurations where a distributed network successfully mitigates dynamic threats (anomalies) using cooperative signaling (VOC RF packets) under extreme energy constraints.
